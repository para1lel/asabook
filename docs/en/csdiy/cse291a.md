> Is this... some kind of prank?

When I was preparing for internship interviews, I looked around for AI systems courses. Stanford's [CS336](https://cs336.stanford.edu/) was very popular at the time, so I tried following it for a while. Then I could not even write a tokenizer, and gave up 😦

Looking back recently, I feel that the course is not very human-shaped. It is called LLM from Scratch, but it is not basic at all: there is a huge amount of scaffold code, and the setting is quite abstract. The lectures take a high-level view, while the assignments are full of traps. The content also leans more toward algorithms; at my previous company, people working on this kind of thing were moved to the algorithm department. I might as well look for courses that focus more directly on algorithms and applications.

After filtering through [csdiy](https://csdiy.wiki/%E6%B7%B1%E5%BA%A6%E7%94%9F%E6%88%90%E6%A8%A1%E5%9E%8B/roadmap/), I found a few courses that seem more approachable:

- [CSE 291A](https://haoailab.com/cse291-s26/) is a short course taught by [Prof. Hao Zhang](https://global.sjtu.edu.cn/announcement/view/2231). It starts from basic deep learning and computation graphs, then moves on to various frontier topics around LLM training and inference. The assignments look fairly foundational and good for practice, and the course also comes with rich notes, videos, and reading materials.
- [CMU 15-779](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~zhihaoj2/15-779/schedule.html) is a paper-reading course, quite similar to Prof. Xu's computer networking course I took before. For self-study, though, I can only read the slides and papers, so it works better as supplementary reading. If there is time, I can also look at [LLMSys-PaperList](https://github.com/AmberLJC/LLMSys-PaperList).
- [MIT 6.5940](https://efficientml.ai) feels more like a foundational undergraduate course. It approaches the topic from an application perspective and does not go too deep into the systems side. The last offering was in 2024, so the material is a little dated. The nice part is that it has an active [YouTube channel](https://live.efficientml.ai), and the assignments are five code-completion notebooks that look pretty fun. Maybe I can play with it after the Fall 2026 offering starts.
- Speaking of YouTube, [GPU MODE](https://www.youtube.com/@GPUMODE) is a community focused on GPU programming. One day I can give it a taste 🥚.

Also, since I recently left my job and no longer have servers to use, I will mostly read theory courses for now. Turns out it was right to learn from the math-department Jiahaos and become a "terminology person."
