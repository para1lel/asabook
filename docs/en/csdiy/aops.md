> A great discovery solves a great problem, but there is a grain of discovery in the solution of any problem.

What makes mathematical writing good? What kind of writing can reveal the ideas behind the symbols and help the reader actually understand them?  
Many textbooks and blog posts simply list definitions and theorems. Reading them feels like sailing without a map, which is obviously far from ideal.  
A solution write-up is at least a slightly better form: it gives us a lighthouse to follow, so we are less likely to get lost in the details.

A good solution should read like a complete story. It begins with a problem whose background is clear and interesting, then raises and resolves one question after another until we finally reach the destination. Two points are especially important:

- Any concept outside the current scope should be explained. The explanation can be brief or detailed depending on the situation.
- Any step that is not obvious should come with its motivation and background. At minimum, the write-up must convince its own author.

The following sections will be written around this idea. The topics may vary widely, but the principle should stay the same.
